## Egypt and its generated household

The Egyptian Ministry places unification and Memphis within the Early Dynastic period, approximately 3100–2686 BC. This supports the state and capital around 3000 BC. It does not identify a king for that exact year. The authored king, spouse, family groups, names and relationships therefore carry generated-origin claims. The ruler's title is **kung**. The religious context draws on the Met's account of early Horus names; religious offices and numerical resources follow game rules. [Egyptian Ministry, Early Dynastic Period](https://egymonuments.gov.eg/historical-periods/early-dynastic-period/), [Met, Stela of King Raneb](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/545799).

The Memphis marker copies Getty's modern Mit Rahina reference point, `[31.25, 29.85]`, with attribution and ODC-By terms. The Swedish note describes its approximate position beside the ancient site. It makes no claim to an excavated early palace. [Getty TGN 7001186](https://www.getty.edu/vow/TGNFullDisplay?english=Y&find=Heidelberg&nation=&place=inhabited+place&subjectid=7001186), [Getty vocabulary licensing](https://www.getty.edu/research-conservation/tools-databases/vocabularies/data-services/).

The conversion retains a **selective Egyptian core**, intersecting upstream feature 19 with the developer-authored box `[-180, 24.2, 180, 90]` (longitude/latitude). The southern cut is a conservative display limit north of the Elephantine/First Cataract frontier region. It is not an attested ancient boundary. The remaining outer edges retain the publisher's world-scale approximation. This partial representation may omit actual Egyptian control; excluded land remains unresolved, without implying that it was empty or non-Egyptian. The exact transformation, original geometry hash and scoped evidence remain in polity, region, review and asset records. Player context, coverage notes and the selection card explain the partial coverage.

This narrower mapping follows an extent-specific review. The Met dates Egyptian domination between the first and third cataracts to approximately 2800–2200 BC. MPI places Egyptian settlements in Lower Nubia around 3100 BC, while UCL places the A-Group's end around 3000 BC and labels the following Lower Nubian record unknown. These period treatments disagree; settlement, trade and raids do not establish continuous ownership of the source polygon's entire southern reach at the bookmark. [Met, Sudan chronology](https://www.metmuseum.org/pt/toah/ht/02/afs.html), [MPI, opening two paragraphs](https://nubianrockart.mpiwg-berlin.mpg.de/nubia/history_archaeology/overview.html), [UCL, Nubia timeline](https://www.ucl.ac.uk/museums-static/digitalegypt/nubia/timeline.html).

Moeller's Nile Valley/Delta settlement discussion and Elephantine frontier-region evidence support the broad core context. Her fortress figure spans the later First Dynasty to early Second Dynasty; it provides no exact 3000 BC frontier or clipping vertices. The developer's mask is an explicit generalization based on this limited regional evidence. Geometry regressions retain Memphis and withhold Egyptian ownership at the southern diagnostic points for Buhen, Semna and Soleb; those checks do not prove ancient control. The package does not assign Ur's polygon to Uruk, select Menes from a legend or backdate later titulary. [Moeller, chapter 4, sections 4.1 and 4.2.6, Figures 4.1 and 4.15](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/archaeology-of-urbanism-in-ancient-egypt/origins-of-urban-society/7827A646AACBAEEEFCC7CF442E904D2B).

## John, Isabella and composite allegiance

Westminster Abbey dates John's accession to 1199 and his marriage to Isabella of Angoulême to 24 August 1200. The chosen bookmark follows that marriage and her October coronation. John and Isabella have sourced identity and spouse claims. John's 1167 birth year remains uncertain; Isabella's circa-1188 birth becomes an explicitly estimated `1188-01-01` engine date. The package adds no future children and no invented royal parentage. Generated court families represent gameplay households. [Westminster Abbey, John](https://www.westminster-abbey.org/abbey-commemorations/royals/john), [Nicholas Vincent, Oxford DNB, Isabella](https://academic.oup.com/oxford-dictionary-of-national-biography/reference/63486/reference-article-abstract/580767093).

Windsor serves as a gameplay seat because the Royal Collection Trust describes royal accommodation there before 1200. Its point uses Getty's Windsor inhabited-place record, `[-0.6, 51.4833]`, rather than the earlier restrictive Heritage Gateway coordinate lead. The point marks the town; the household's location on the chosen day remains unknown. [Royal Collection Trust, Windsor origins](https://www.rct.uk/visit/windsor-castle/who-built-windsor-castle), [Getty TGN 7011919](https://www.getty.edu/vow/TGNFullDisplay?english=N&find=&nation=Uganda&place=&subjectid=7011919).

Ruling: **Johns riken (angevinskt välde)** denotes composite simulation control. Parliament identifies his English crown and French possessions; the National Archives explains the French vassal relationship. The published Historical Research article, available through Cardiff's institutional repository, describes Arthur as John's liegeman under the 1200 settlement. The continental geometry therefore includes a Brittany/Arthur qualification. This model does not claim uniform direct administration. If the abstraction proves inadequate, a later revision must split separately sourced jurisdictions. [UK Parliament, John's possessions](https://www.parliament.uk/about/living-heritage/evolutionofparliament/originsofparliament/birthofparliament/overview/magnacarta/magnacartahow/), [National Archives, printed pages 13–14](https://cdn.nationalarchives.gov.uk/documents/education/magna-carta-preparation-pack.pdf), [Historical Research, printed page 423, notes 123–126](https://orca.cardiff.ac.uk/id/eprint/165780/1/BM%20HR.pdf).

The authored mapping accepts both components of upstream Angevin feature 275. Regression points include Windsor, Rouen, Bordeaux, Angers and Rennes; negative points include Edinburgh, Gwynedd, Cardiff, Dublin, Connacht, Paris and Toulouse. The source contains no Irish component. The package discloses that omission rather than inventing Irish control geometry. These rounded points test coarse placement. They are not historical settlement coordinates or proof of exact local borders.

## Physical land and geometry authority

The independent physical backdrop uses Natural Earth's 1:50m land at commit `ca96624a56bd078437bca8184e78163e5039ad19`. It restores Pacific land absent from the historical polygons. This is generalized present-day land, including its omissions at that scale; it does not reconstruct ancient coastlines, river courses or settlement. [Natural Earth land](https://www.naturalearthdata.com/downloads/50m-physical-vectors/50m-land/), [publisher terms](https://www.naturalearthdata.com/about/terms-of-use/).

The converter subtracts accepted political polygons from that land and marks the remainder **Okänd historisk kontroll**. These computational pieces receive no administrative borders or polity records. Unknown control does not mean uninhabited or available land. Tests check that a single reviewed region owns each seat in the shared placement pipeline and that unknown pieces create no polity labels.

Both flat and globe presentation intersect polar geometry with the approximately ±85.0511° Mercator latitude band before tiling. This prevents vertex clamping from making the Antarctic polygon self-intersect. MapLibre extends boundary-touching land to the pole on the globe; arbitrary beyond-band detail and isolated polar caps are not preserved by this renderer. Original scenario geometry, IDs and exports remain unchanged. The flat repair at `39448ec` was followed by a globe repair at `2066a55`; both passed scoped review. On `2066a55`, the large globe wedges disappeared in the inspected view. A straight Antarctic shading seam and a small blue gap near the pole remain as deferred visual minors; their cause is unconfirmed.

Historical geometry remains authoritative at close zoom, even if an imported region ID matches a modern tile. Historical map rendering, flag resolution, country-list assembly and place search suppress modern fallback. A missing historical city asset leaves an empty city layer. Modern saves retain their existing behavior. The map selection card labels unknown control and marks accepted regions as approximate.

The conversion quarantines AD 1200 features 214 (Goryeo, self-intersection) and 240 (degenerate unnamed geometry). It assigns no replacement ownership. Natural Earth keeps the land visible. The generated release has valid polygons under an independent Shapely audit; runtime tests also cover holes, closed rings, dateline component handling and source-based placement. ## Schema and court contract

`validateHistoricalScenario({ game, world, regions, assetManifest }, { date, polityId })` performs pure structural checks. Success returns `{ ok: true, contentId, contentVersion, date, polityId }`. Failures return a code and one Swedish refusal. It verifies canonical supported dates, exact bookmark availability, source references, unique IDs, region owners, world overrides, settlement placement, playable courts, identity origin, claim certainty and asset metadata. It never treats a URL as proof that a human reviewed its claim.

`world.historicalScenario` schema version 1 contains:

- Content identity, exact bookmark and calendar; optional researched validity endpoints require source IDs.
- Sources with title, author/institution, URL, locator, retrieval date, original chronology, uncertainty and reuse terms.
- Polities, playable polity IDs, settlements, continental coverage notes, per-source-feature review and the asset manifest.
- `courtSeeds[polityId]` with `people`, `families`, `religions`, `offices` and `cities` arrays; `rulerId` and `seatId` link those records.
- Claim records `{ status: 'sourced' | 'uncertain' | 'generated', sourceIds, noteSv }`. A documented person still has generated mechanics and may have uncertain dates.

Each person carries `identityOrigin`, a Swedish origin label, family/religion IDs, authored birthdate, `legalParentIds`, `spouseIds`, claim metadata and generated mechanical inputs. Supplied birthdays require `claims.bornAt`; nonempty relationships require their matching `claims.legalParentIds` or `claims.spouseIds`. Birthdays use canonical game-calendar syntax without year zero and cannot follow the selected date. They may precede the campaign range's 3300 BC lower bound. Spouses must link reciprocally; family heads, family religions, religion leaders and city family/region/settlement links must resolve within the authored court. The accepted Task 3 factory converts these seeds to the shared character engine shape. Court provenance describes public authorship; it must not receive private loyalty, secrets or other hidden runtime facts. Family grouping, succession, official roles and resources remain authored mechanics. A contested settlement requires no forced owner: use `polityId: null`, `placement: 'contested'`, competing polity IDs, a source and an explanatory note. It cannot silently become the controlled court seat.

The asset manifest requires schema version 1 and matching `contentId`/`contentVersion`, with both `regionsGeojson` and `citiesGeojson` descriptors. Its source and transformation records describe the matching package. The pure validator checks these relationships and record shapes; release checks independently verify serialized-byte hashes.

## Reproduction and component terms

Run `node scripts/build-historical-scenarios.mjs` to rebuild, or append `--check` to compare committed bytes without writing. The converter uses local pinned inputs and no clock or network. Each bookmark contains `scenario.json`, `regions.geojson`, `cities.geojson` and `manifest.json`. Input hashes cover upstream files and authored review inputs; asset hashes cover compact JSON followed by LF. Source-feature hashes use `JSON.stringify(geometry)` in the pinned file's property order. Git attributes preserve input and output bytes across platforms.

A released package's `generator`, `sourceFiles` and `authoredInputs` manifest fields record the build that released it. `server/seed/historical/released-provenance.json` holds them as frozen data, and the build copies them into that package's manifest. A package without an entry computes the three fields from the current files. `--check` proves that the current generator reproduces every other byte of each released package.

Historical Basemaps uses commit `4b5039e50d8d2464b065bd76ad6086cd4597108d`. Its root license supplies GPL version 3; its README preserves collection credits and warns that reconstruction remains incomplete. The release includes the original selected GeoJSON, full license, README and contributor notice, plus modification descriptions. This records the publisher's stated terms and source chain; it does not certify historical precision or the rights of each earlier collection contributor. [Pinned upstream source and notices](https://github.com/aourednik/historical-basemaps/tree/4b5039e50d8d2464b065bd76ad6086cd4597108d).

The bundle's CC BY record covers authored content. `componentLicenses` identifies embedded GPL geometry, Natural Earth public-domain physical data and Getty ODC-By location data. Both stores retain these records and asset provenance in world state. JSON exports preserve them; ZIP and suggestion exports include component notices and corresponding source entries. Re-importing and exporting on either adapter preserves original source hashes, license text and transformation records. Ordinary authored scenarios retain their existing CC BY behavior. Application AGPL notices remain in place.

The year popup and the chronicle segment `I verklighetens historia` exist and read one approved catalog. The catalog ships empty until the research round, so no popup appears yet and the segment shows its empty text. The segment is available for historical campaigns, lists the closed years newest first and notes that the research ends at year 1200. Annual historical-event research, additional dates and more detailed political frontiers remain separate work. This release does not fill those gaps with generated history.

## Model context and alternate outcomes

Task 4 passed its independent review. The existing interactive and jump prompt paths read a whitelist from the visible character presentation: content identity/version, starting bookmark and era, curated setting/calendar/coverage/polity/mechanics notes, current date, current polity and current ruler. People carry their public generated/documented label. Prompts omit nested source records, court seeds and person claim provenance. Ongoing campaigns can pass AD 1200; prompt construction applies the generic calendar without imposing the new-start range.

The Egyptian note identifies the developer-selected and clipped partial core, the unresolved southern control and the absence of an attested exact frontier. The model receives this qualification with the setting. Historical research supplies starting context; the engine retains authority over dates, rulers, borders and stored outcomes. Recorded future events do not prescribe campaign outcomes. Annual historical-reference events remain a separate feature.

Ruling: for a validated historical character campaign, prompt assembly replaces the whole named `[Real History Is the Default]` section in loaded, frozen or custom jump templates with engine-owned guidance for alternate outcomes. Replacement stops at the next section boundary. It preserves other author sections and leaves nonhistorical templates unchanged. The cost is that author edits inside that named section are replaced. Authors must resolve arbitrary contradictory instructions elsewhere in a custom template; the engine does not heuristically delete unrelated text. A template without the named section still receives the historical guidance through the live character context.

These checks cover captured interactive requests and assembled jump prompts without live model calls. Task 5 integrated historical defaults and start guards; Task 6 browser evidence is accepted with the limits recorded in the play script.

Task 4 ruling extension: historical mode also replaces three exact shipped sentences outside that named section: the real-history default in the authority list, the automatic jump requirement that unprevented real events still happen, and the event-writing instruction to retain real historical details. Their replacements refer to the sourced starting context, current campaign records and the player's response to campaign events. Surrounding authority and pacing rules stay in place. The cost applies to authors who retained those exact shipped sentences; other custom wording remains the author's responsibility. Nonhistorical final prompts retain the original wording.
