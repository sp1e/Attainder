For feature 80, the Met describes a late-fifteenth-century tributary empire reaching both coasts and coastal Guatemala. It identifies autonomous Tlaxcala and resistance by the Tarascan kingdom. The unchanged polygon includes a Tlaxcala diagnostic point. The western Pacific tail through the Colima area to about 105.5° W lacks support in this material. No opened source fixes its northern edge at 22.896° N. The source feature ends in the southeast around 93.74° W and omits part of the wider tribute sphere described toward Guatemala. Smith's Figure 5 concerns 1519 and distinguishes tributary and strategic provinces from trade evidence; it supplies a later comparison. The reviewer must retain its later date. See [The Met, overview](https://www.metmuseum.org/toah/ht/08/canm.html) and [Smith, Long-Distance Trade under the Aztec Empire](https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/S0956536100000183).

For feature 83, Smithsonian's expansion figure distinguishes Tupac's pre-1493 expansion from additions during Huayna's whole reign. The source feature reaches 37.283° S, south of the museum figure's Maule limit. Pärssinen distinguishes permanent traces near Cachapoal from reported limits and contacts farther south. The eastern edge across Bolivia and northwestern Argentina has regional support with no verified frontier or uniform control. See [Smithsonian's expansion figure](https://americanindian.si.edu/inkaroad/img/inkauniverse/cusco/builders-inka-expansion-map.jpg), [Pärssinen, p. 614](https://repositoriodigital.bnp.gob.pe/bnp/recursos/2/html/el-imperio-inka/614/) and [northern chronology, pp. 809–810](https://repositoriodigital.bnp.gob.pe/bnp/recursos/2/html/el-imperio-inka/809/).
