# 2400–2301 BC: supplementary events

This supplement adds two events, three sources, ten English event fields and six Swedish source-panel fields. Both events concern southern Mesopotamia and retain approximate dates. Other regions and empty years remain research gaps.

## Evidence and chronology

The [Getty exhibition image sheet](https://www.getty.edu/press/pdfs/Getty-Mesopotamia-Press-Images.pdf), page 8, item 25, dates a cone recording Urukagina's reforms to about 2350 BC. This museum date does not establish an exact enactment year. [Schrakamp and Zólyomi's study](https://www.degruyterbrill.com/document/doi/10.1515/aofo-2025-2006/pdf), sections 1–2 and 13, distinguishes the inscriptions' promises from evidence of implementation. Archival comparison supports removal of the maškim official from debt and penalty collection; it does not prove that every royal reform claim took effect. Their 2335–2315 BC archival context covers three rulers collectively, rather than dating this particular cone.

[Goodman and colleagues](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/gea.70027) place Lugalzagesi's attack on Lagash around 2350 BC in their introduction. Section 5.1 also gives alternative reign chronologies: about 2358–2334 BC under the Middle Chronology and 2294–2270 BC under the Low Chronology. Those reign intervals do not supply an individual attack year. The separate sediment date of 2390 ± 220 BC is not a battle date. Deliberate flooding remains the authors' hypothesis.
