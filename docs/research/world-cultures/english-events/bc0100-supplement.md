## Dates and source limits

The Metropolitan Museum of Art places the development of glassblowing early in the first century BCE, while the Corning Museum of Glass places it near the middle. The dataset's century-wide numeric bounds contain those verbal estimates. They do not date the innovation to 100 or 1 BCE or describe a process lasting the entire century. Corning supplies the hollow-tube explanation; Met describes faster, more versatile manufacture.

The wreck record retains the museum's approximate 60–50 BCE estimate, WHOI's 70–60 BCE estimate, and the Antikythera Mechanism Research Project FAQ's two differently bounded estimates, 80–50 and 85–60 BCE. The combined 85–50 BCE span represents alternatives, not continuous sinking. Cargo and timber dates are separate. No precise sailing route, proven storm cause or mechanism manufacture date is asserted.

Desanges explicitly dates Juba II's accession to 25 BCE using numismatic and literary evidence. Only the public HTML text of his article's first page was inspected; its ancient references and coins were not independently examined. The dataset performs no regnal conversion and does not adopt disputed territorial boundaries.

The six retained sources were inspected as public HTML text. Images, underlying archaeological studies and ancient works cited by modern authors were not independently inspected. Related institutional accounts are not presented as independent chronological corroboration. Reference-only metadata describes the dataset's use, not a replacement for any source's licence.
