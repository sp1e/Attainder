## Evidence and chronology

Each event cites one Key Events entry of the Metropolitan Museum of Art's Heilbrunn Timeline of Art History, from the pages "Great Britain and Ireland, 500–1000 A.D.", "Western North Africa, 1–500 A.D.", "Egypt, 1–500 A.D.", "Korea, 1–500 A.D.", "China, 1–500 A.D.", "Western and Central Europe, 1–500 A.D.". Each source record names the entry by its date phrase and opening words. The research session read the page text only; no images, object records or primary editions were examined. The Met dates most entries with "ca.", and the records keep that as approximate dates or ranges, with `noteEn` where the entry leaves the year of a single act open.

## Review and withholding

A fresh Claude subagent received only the delivered files, opened every cited page and checked each field against its entry. It corrected one selection note, which called the Vandals a kingdom where the entry calls them a Germanic tribe. All corrections are applied. Every page loaded; the site redirects each URL to the same page under `/pt/toah/ht/`.

Hypatia's death (415) was withheld because the entry gives one sentence only. Attila's westward advance (434), the Angle and Saxon settlement of about 430, the royal tombs at Monte Albán (about 450) and the Ajanta paintings were left for a later pass. Duplicate checks ran against the runtime catalog and every English dataset in this folder, by id and by historical episode.

Ruling: one institutional timeline supports each record, and each summary stays within its entry. This supplement does not establish complete regional or annual coverage. Compatible English runtime support remains pending; the gameplay catalog, legacy locales and coverage counts remain unchanged.
