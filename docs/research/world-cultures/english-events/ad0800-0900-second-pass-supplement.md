# AD 800–999: water supply and personal status

This second pass examines Kairouan's Aghlabid reservoirs and Gwangjong's review of enslaved people's status. They are separate regional developments in infrastructure and government intervention.

The Tunisian heritage agency gives 860–862 for construction of the pools. Archnet also supplies that range, while its narrative gives 862 and its Hijri labels differ internally. The record preserves these distinctions without calculating another date. Settling, storage and drawing facilities supported urban water supply, caravans and livestock. No claim of hydraulic invention or permanent relief from drought is made.

The Korean specialist entry dates the status-review policy to 956. It concerns people who had previously been free and were enslaved during warfare or by local magnates. Restoration to commoner status is distinguished from universal emancipation. The source's later reversals are retained as a limitation, not converted into an invented end year.

All three cited sources were read as public HTML. They are modern institutional or scholarly accounts; no original building inscription or medieval legal manuscript was independently inspected. The related Gwangjong biography corroborated the year but contributes no separate source claim to the dataset.

The reservoirs differ from the existing Kairouan mosque reconstruction; the status review differs from Goryeo's examination reform in 958. Cheonghaejin's maritime base and Feng Tao's printing programme were excluded because they already have catalog records. Earlier regional access and chronology holds remain unresolved.
