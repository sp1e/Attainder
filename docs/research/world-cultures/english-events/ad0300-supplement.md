# AD 300–399 English research supplement

The independently reviewed [dataset](ad0300-supplement.json) contains two events and three sources: the end of known royal pyramid burials at Meroe and the construction of the Adosada platform against Teotihuacan's Feathered Serpent Pyramid. Both dates are approximate, around 350 CE. Both records retain `historicalStatus: uncertain` and regional importance 2.

Meroe's burial sequence is kept separate from political collapse and any proposed Aksumite conquest. The Teotihuacan record preserves political and ritual interpretations as alternatives and leaves damage to other façades undated. The Arqueología Mexicana citation is a public excerpt from a 2008 guide; the complete issue was not accessed. The other sources are Kuckertz's UCLA encyclopedia article and Barnes's Met essay.

A fresh independent reviewer read all three cited passages and all ten English display fields. It requested one exact correction: removing “Later” before the reference to damage whose timing is unknown. The original candidate and HOLD report remain preserved. A second independent review confirmed that this was the only changed data field, read all ten final English fields, verified every field hash and source reference, and returned PASS. Both processes exited successfully.

The complete runtime catalog used for the author's deduplication contained 873 events and 801 sources at commit `04ca6e0c0667325b0ca2816ccb31b5f136fe5c7c`. Existing related entries concern earlier Teotihuacan development, the establishment of royal burial at Meroe and Roman campaigns. No separate English JSON dataset existed at that initial check. Integration also checked the two-record BC2200 English supplement: identifiers, sources and historical episodes do not overlap.

The Tooth Relic arrival lead remains withheld. Its earlier review lacked an explicit era for a cited reign. New publications provide differing chronological contexts for the arrival tradition and the royal festival; no exact arrival date has been silently harmonized. A Cham inscription lead encountered a robot-verification gate, and a Samudragupta inscription lead did not yield a securely established date.

The investigation stopped the OpenEdition robot-verification route and an INAH route after a TLS failure. The independently published Arqueología Mexicana excerpt is not an alternate copy of the failed INAH page. Other failed routes returned technical timeouts, DNS failure, a missing PDF or gateway errors. None supplied evidence for the retained records.

All new event text is English. These records await runtime support and change no gameplay event, locale or coverage totals. No source images or extended source text are redistributed.
