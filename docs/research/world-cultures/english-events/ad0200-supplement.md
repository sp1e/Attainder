# AD200–299 English research supplement

The [dataset](ad0200-supplement.json) contains four independently approved events, six references and twenty English display fields. Source and final reviews returned PASS without required changes. The final review checked all twenty display fields, four complete selection notes and thirty-seven frozen inputs; both review processes exited successfully. The runtime catalog remains at 873 events and 801 sources. The supplement requires importer support before it can appear in the game; runtime coverage and existing locale records are unchanged.

The selection covers Bambata pottery dispersal around 200, Cao Pi's takeover of the Eastern Han throne in 220, Ardashir's establishment of the Sasanian dynasty in 224, and Wu's mission to Funan. It fills specific gaps rather than completing the century or providing one event per year.

Huffman's public abstract attributes the spread of Bambata pottery to hunter-gatherer groups around 200. That interpretation is retained as uncertain and kept separate from his later date around 350 for mixed-farming communities. The full paper requires a subscription and was not accessed. The issue is dated 2005; the publisher also displays a 2021 web-publication date.

The two dynastic transitions use museum accounts. Cao Pi's takeover is separate from his nine-rank recruitment reform already in the runtime catalog. Tokyo National Museum and the Met agree on the 220 boundary but use different end years, 280 and 265, for the wider Three Kingdoms period. That difference is preserved as a source-review caveat; the entry does not adopt a period end date. Ardashir's foundation year does not imply that he conquered every Parthian territory immediately. Neither entry supplies an unsupported month or day.

Chen's public English abstract reports three datings for Zhu Ying and Kang Tai's mission to Funan: 229, 243–252, and his own proposal of 244–247. The full bounds are retained as alternatives, not the duration of one journey. The Japanese full article, underlying chronicles and other historians' works were not independently examined. The source title is the published English title on J-STAGE. A subsequent bibliographic check at CiNii met a robot challenge and was stopped.

Several leads remain withheld. Iranica's opening passages were readable, but later targeted passages timed out and exact-URL downloads returned 403; no alternative copies were pursued. Dura-Europos's Christian building has differing phase dates and disputed construction evidence, so no precise conversion date is added. Valerian's capture was not included because the opened ANU record and an unread Getty search lead supplied differing years. Broad Moche, Kangju, Nagarjunakonda and Pacific-settlement leads did not establish additional bounded episodes in this pass. Previously denied Aksum, Teotihuacan, Three Kingdoms and Taupo routes were not retried.

Deduplication uses private copies of the 873-event runtime catalog and eighteen English datasets, including the separately prepared BC2300 supplement. That comparison does not approve or integrate the other datasets. References identify the passages used; no source prose or images are redistributed. Historical uncertainty and access limits remain part of the delivery.
