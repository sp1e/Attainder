# Persepolis ration payments

This supplement proposes one English research record with two cited sources and five display fields, plus its selection note. It concerns an administrative practice documented in the Persepolis Treasury records. It does not date the invention of wages or the foundation of a treasury.

Hallock's public chapter summary dates the records to 492–458 BCE, from Darius I's year 30 through Artaxerxes I's year 7. Garrison's publisher description dates the archive to 492–457 BCE. Both complete spans are retained. Their combined bounds are not a claim that payments occurred continuously or that a reform began in 492 and ended in 457. The earlier Fortification archive is not reassigned to this window.

The payment claim follows Hallock's description of silver replacing part, and occasionally all, of rations. Garrison's description supports the archive's chronology. Both accounts concern the same archive; their agreement is not two independent ancient testimonies. Only the public publisher summaries were inspected. Neither the access-controlled Cambridge chapter nor the Garrison book, original tablets or photographs were read.
