# Ross Island copper production

This English research supplement proposes one technological episode with four source records: copper mining and production at Ross Island and its supply connections with Ireland and Britain. The event retains the published c. 2400–1900/1800 BC chronology, the 1995 work-camp report's 2400–1800 BC interval and the earlier 1992 report's 2400–2100 cal BC. The approximate start is not an exact foundation year; the full span does not assert continuous annual operation.

The claim concerns an excavated production site and regional metal circulation. It makes no claim about a single year of metal's arrival, ethnic migration, a copper monopoly or the invention of tin bronze. Two furnaces tentatively assigned to the Early Christian period in the 1995 report are excluded. The British Beaker burial record already in the English research collection concerns a different mortuary episode.

The sources read were two public excavation reports, the opening-page text of O’Brien’s 2023 chapter as publicly extracted on Academia.edu, and section 2 of Carey and colleagues' 2019 Internet Archaeology article. The chapter text has interleaved columns; no PDF image was visually inspected. The later accounts draw on the Ross Island excavation literature and are not independent radiocarbon datasets. An Oxford book-chapter route returned a non-retryable inaccessible redirect and was stopped. Its search preview is not evidence for the event.
