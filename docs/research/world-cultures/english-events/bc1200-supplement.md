# Ugarit's destruction: chronology and survival

The [dataset](bc1200-supplement.json) contains one approved event, four sources and five English display fields, at importance 2.

This English research supplement contains one event and four references. It records extensive destruction at Ugarit and the end of its Late Bronze Age political existence. The event is separate from the existing Egyptian battle against the Sea Peoples and the Greek palace destructions.

The chronology remains uncertain. Drower's 1975 chapter summary gives about 1200 BCE; Callot and Yon's archaeological discussion gives around 1190/1180. Callot's later discussion treats 1180 as indicative and refers to estimates around 1190–1185. The record's 1200–1180 bounds preserve these differing approximations and do not describe a continuous twenty-year destruction. Neither an exact date nor a single versus multiple sequence of destructive episodes is established.

The attackers are not identified with certainty. Callot's discussion of reoccupation also qualifies the Met overview's categorical statement that Ugarit was never resettled. The entry does not assign a universal cause to the Bronze Age collapse or describe the whole site as permanently deserted.

The evidence comprises a public Cambridge chapter summary, an extracted-text archaeological chapter from the French mission, the publicly readable first page of Callot's later article, and the Met's short essay. No claim is made to have read the gated Cambridge chapter, all pages of the later article, or PDF page images. The mission PDF required a normal same-URL download after the web tool's size limit. Sources are referenced only; no images or extended source passages are redistributed.

Sanxingdui and Palau leads were withheld after chronology and access limitations; a separate SBL book also returned an access denial. Those routes were not retried through alternate copies. This bounded supplement leaves regional gaps open and does not mark a century complete.
