# AD 1300–1399: English research supplement

## Evidence and chronology

The UNESCO nomination identifies the Terengganu inscription as Malay in Jawi script and supplies a 1303 date. Buang's scholarly book review reports the competing 1308 reading and Blagden's older 1303–1387 range. The record retains all three alternatives as disputed dating of one inscription. Its legal provisions support the narrower claim of proclamation; they do not establish the first arrival of Islam or uniform enforcement. The book review was read directly; the underlying monograph was not.

Hamburg University's transcription and translation dates the incomplete Canggu charter to July–August 1358. Hayam Wuruk's provisions concern ferrymen's privileges and obligations, including revenue claims, a festival payment and specified protections. These are normative regulations, not evidence of universal enforcement. The edition is labelled an unproofread first draft. Its supplied month range and original calendar wording remain visible without an independently calculated modern day.
