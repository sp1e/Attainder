# Glassworking in Egypt around 1500 BCE

The [dataset](bc1500-supplement.json) contains one independently approved event, two sources and five English display fields. The event has importance 1.

This English research supplement concerns the adoption of glass as a craft material in Egypt around 1500 BCE. It distinguishes that development from the older faience tradition and from the later production of the museum vessel used to introduce the subject.

The Walters Art Museum places the emergence of true-glass production around 1500 BCE and describes western Asian technological transfer and early imports as hypotheses. Nicholson and Jackson also place glass's introduction around 1500 BCE, while distinguishing possible local manufacture under Thutmose III, whose reign they give as 1479–1425 BCE. The record preserves the approximate introduction and the uncertainty surrounding the first local manufacture. It does not turn a reign boundary into a workshop's founding year or place the later Amarna industry in 1500 BCE.

The selected evidence is a museum's public historical discussion and an academic article available through Cardiff University. Relevant PDF text was read without visual page verification. No images or extended source passages are redistributed. The episode concerns a technological adoption, rather than the manufacture of a single surviving vessel or the invention of glass worldwide.
