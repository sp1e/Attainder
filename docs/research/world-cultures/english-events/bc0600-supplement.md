## Evidence and chronology

Each event cites one Key Events entry of the Metropolitan Museum of Art's Heilbrunn Timeline of Art History, from the pages "Central and Southern Andes, 1000 B.C.–1 A.D.", "Eastern Europe and Scandinavia, 1000 B.C.–1 A.D.", "Mexico, 1000 B.C.–1 A.D.", "Iran, 1000 B.C.–1 A.D.", "Iberian Peninsula, 1000 B.C.–1 A.D.". Each source record names the entry by its date phrase and opening words. The research session read the page text only; no images, object records or primary editions were examined. The Met dates most entries with "ca.", and the records keep that as approximate dates or ranges, with `noteEn` where the entry leaves the year of a single act open.

## Review and withholding

A fresh Claude subagent received only the delivered files, opened every cited page and checked each field against its entry. It corrected three records: Kuntur Wasi's title called the buried person a ruler, the Animal Style text said "often" where the entry says some of the best-known works, and the Aleria summary placed Tartessos from a different entry. All corrections are applied. Every page loaded; the site redirects each URL to the same page under `/pt/toah/ht/`.

The Phocaean trading station at Emporion (about 600 BCE) was withheld: the entry places Emporion in Tartessos in south Spain, and no second source was opened to check that placement. Baltic face urns (600–400 BCE) and the Manga'asi pottery of Vanuatu (600 BCE–1200 CE) were left for a later pass. Duplicate checks ran against the runtime catalog and every English dataset in this folder, by id and by historical episode.

Ruling: one institutional timeline supports each record, and each summary stays within its entry. This supplement does not establish complete regional or annual coverage. Compatible English runtime support remains pending; the gameplay catalog, legacy locales and coverage counts remain unchanged.
